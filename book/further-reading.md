# Further Reading

The shelves this book leans on, by discipline — with the bridge books last, which are the rarest kind.

## Scripture and its craft

- E. W. Bullinger, **Figures of Speech Used in the Bible** (1898) — the great catalog of 200+ biblical figures; the seed-link of this repository. [Read online](https://levendwater.org/books/figures_of_speech/index.htm). ([Ch. 6](06-figures-of-speech-divine-compression.md))
- Robert Lowth, **Lectures on the Sacred Poetry of the Hebrews** (1753) — the codification of Hebrew parallelism.
- Augustine, **On Christian Doctrine** (*De Doctrina Christiana*) — sign theory and hermeneutics, fourth century; arguably the first semiotics text. ([Ch. 2](02-the-image-and-the-icon.md))
- Meredith Kline, **The Structure of Biblical Authority** (1972) — covenant as treaty-document; the two tablets as duplicate copies. ([Ch. 7](07-law-as-code-covenant-as-protocol.md))
- Paul D. Wegner, **The Journey from Texts to Translations** — an accessible survey of how the Bible was transmitted, copied, and translated. ([Ch. 5](05-the-canon-an-error-corrected-channel.md))
- Lamin Sanneh, **Translating the Message** (1989) — Christianity as a translated and translating faith. ([Ch. 4](04-babel-when-protocols-break.md))

## Information science and its history

- Claude Shannon, **A Mathematical Theory of Communication** (1948) — the founding paper; short, readable, and explicit about bracketing meaning. ([Ch. 1](01-in-the-beginning-was-information.md), [5](05-the-canon-an-error-corrected-channel.md))
- Warren Weaver, **Recent Contributions to the Mathematical Theory of Communication** (1949) — the three levels: technical, semantic, effectiveness.
- Norbert Wiener, **The Human Use of Human Beings** (1950) — cybernetics turned to ask what communication does to persons.
- James Gleick, **The Information: A History, a Theory, a Flood** (2011) — the panoramic popular history.
- Herbert Simon, "Designing Organizations for an Information-Rich World" (1971) — the attention-scarcity argument. ([Ch. 9](09-from-data-to-wisdom.md))

## Philosophy

- Plato, **Cratylus** — the first treatise on names. ([Ch. 3](03-naming-the-animals.md))
- J. L. Austin, **How to Do Things with Words** (1962) — performatives and speech acts. ([Ch. 1](01-in-the-beginning-was-information.md))
- Charles Sanders Peirce, selected papers on semiotics — icon, index, symbol. ([Ch. 2](02-the-image-and-the-icon.md))
- Ludwig Wittgenstein, **Tractatus** and **Philosophical Investigations** — meaning, limits of language, language games. ([Ch. 4](04-babel-when-protocols-break.md))
- Karl Popper, **The Logic of Scientific Discovery** — falsifiability. ([Ch. 8](08-prophecy-signal-verification-trust.md))
- John Locke, **An Essay Concerning Human Understanding**, II.27 — personal identity and memory. ([Ch. 12](12-memory-identity-resurrection.md))
- George Lakoff & Mark Johnson, **Metaphors We Live By** (1980) — metaphor as the format of thought. ([Ch. 6](06-figures-of-speech-divine-compression.md))
- Fred Dretske, **Knowledge and the Flow of Information** (1981) — the most rigorous attempt to build epistemology on information theory.
- Luciano Floridi, **Information: A Very Short Introduction** (2010) and **The Philosophy of Information** (2011) — the contemporary philosophy of information. ([Ch. 1](01-in-the-beginning-was-information.md))

## The bridges: faith, technology, and the word

- Walter Ong, **Orality and Literacy** (1982) — how writing restructures consciousness; indispensable background for a religion of the spoken-then-written word.
- Marshall McLuhan, **Understanding Media** (1964) — the medium as message; McLuhan's own thought was deeply shaped by his Catholic faith. ([Ch. 15](15-practicing-technecy.md))
- Jacques Ellul, **The Humiliation of the Word** (1981) — a theologian's defense of the word against the image.
- Neil Postman, **Amusing Ourselves to Death** (1985) and **Technopolis** (1992) — media ecology with prophetic nerve.
- Albert Borgmann, **Holding On to Reality: The Nature of Information at the Turn of the Millennium** (1999) — a philosopher of technology on natural, cultural, and technological information.
- Vern Poythress, **In the Beginning Was the Word: Language — A God-Centered Approach** (2009) — a theology of language by a Westminster theologian with a Harvard mathematics doctorate.
- Noreen Herzfeld, **In Our Image: Artificial Intelligence and the Human Spirit** (2002) — imago Dei and the machines we make in ours. ([Ch. 2](02-the-image-and-the-icon.md), [15](15-practicing-technecy.md))
- John Dyer, **From the Garden to the City** (2011) — an accessible theology of technology by a programmer.

## On reading this list

Qoheleth's warning governs here too: *"of making many books there is no end"* (Ecclesiastes 12:12). Do not read all of these. Pick one bridge book, one primary source (Shannon's paper, or Austin's lectures — both are short), and keep the Canon open beside them. That ratio — more baseline than commentary — is itself one of this book's disciplines ([Ch. 15](15-practicing-technecy.md)).

# 15 · Practicing Technecy in a Digital Age

> *"And he had power to give life unto the image of the beast, that the image of the beast should both speak..."* — Revelation 13:15
>
> *"The medium is the message."* — Marshall McLuhan, *Understanding Media* (1964)

**In this chapter** — *Theology:* faithfulness in a new information environment. *Information science:* the age of generated text and speaking images. *Philosophy:* a rule of life for the craft of the word — which is where this book has been heading all along.

## The old questions return, at scale

Every chapter of this book has been secretly about the present. We now live inside an information environment the biblical writers could not have pictured and yet described with uncanny precision: a flood of words without senders, images without originals, prophets without verification, and — the detail Revelation reserved for its darkest chapter — **images that speak**. Whatever John meant by the beast's talking image, his analysis of it stands: an *eikōn* given the power of speech is the ancient world's limit-case of deception, because speech is the signature of persons — and an image that speaks counterfeits personhood itself. We have built such images at industrial scale, and they are very fluent.

None of this requires panic; it requires *craft*. The questions this moment forces are exactly the ones Scripture trained its readers to ask, and each has had a chapter:

- **Whose word is this?** — authentication of sender, not just accuracy of content ([Chapter 8](08-prophecy-signal-verification-trust.md)). Generated text is the final divorce of fluency from authorship; the Deuteronomy 13 lesson — *impressiveness is not identity* — is now a daily survival skill.
- **What does the representation leave out?** — every feed, profile, and model is a lossy encoding; treating it as the territory is the perennial idolatry ([Chapter 2](02-the-image-and-the-icon.md)).
- **Is there flesh behind the word?** — the new docetism is ambient: relationships, communities, even selves conducted as pure symbol-streams ([Chapter 10](10-the-word-made-flesh.md)). The Word's own trajectory ran the other way: toward flesh, presence, table, touch.
- **What is this medium doing to me?** — McLuhan's question, which is Goodhart's question, which is the Pharisees' question ([Chapter 7](07-law-as-code-covenant-as-protocol.md)): the form of our communication reshapes the communicants, whatever the content says. A feed optimized for engagement is a *liturgy* — a formative repeated practice — whether or not anyone calls it one.

## A rule of life for the craft

Monastic communities answered chaotic environments with a *rule*: a small set of disciplines, practiced on schedule, that shape the practitioner. The materials of this book assemble naturally into such a rule — technecy as practice, one discipline per chapter-cluster:

1. **Keep a canon.** ([Chapters 5](05-the-canon-an-error-corrected-channel.md), [8](08-prophecy-signal-verification-trust.md)) Curate a small set of trusted, verified sources — and one Source — against which the flood is diffed. The Bereans' two postures: receive readily, verify daily. A person with no canon has no baseline, and a person with no baseline can be told anything.
2. **Sabbath the channel.** ([Chapter 9](09-from-data-to-wisdom.md)) Attention is the scarce resource; rate-limit intake on purpose. One day in seven, the manna-gatherers gathered nothing. Scheduled disconnection is not retreat from reality but reattachment to the part of it that doesn't stream.
3. **Cache truth in the body.** ([Chapter 6](06-figures-of-speech-divine-compression.md)) Memorize — psalms, creeds, poetry. What lives only in the cloud is available until it isn't, and shapes you never. The figures of speech were engineered for exactly this storage medium; use them as designed. *"Thy word have I hid in mine heart"* (Psalm 119:11).
4. **Sign what you say.** ([Chapter 11](11-the-body-as-network.md)) Paul authenticated every letter with his own hand. Let your words be traceable to you and costly to you: *"let your yea be yea"* (James 5:12, after Matthew 5:37) is integrity expressed as protocol — a person whose words need no escalating authentication because none of them are forgeries.
5. **Verify in community.** ([Chapters 8](08-prophecy-signal-verification-trust.md), [11](11-the-body-as-network.md)) Private interpretation of a hostile information environment fails the way private interpretation of prophecy fails. Test claims, and your readings of them, against embodied others who know you — the assembly hearing the letter aloud together.
6. **Refuse the speaking image's premise.** ([Chapters 2](02-the-image-and-the-icon.md), [10](10-the-word-made-flesh.md), [12](12-memory-identity-resurrection.md)) Use generated media as tools; do not accept them as *presences*. Reserve the responses owed to persons — trust, confession, love, grief — for beings with flesh, history, and a name in the book of life.
7. **Selah.** (The whole book.) Build in the pause. Between message and reaction, the psalmists planted a word whose probable meaning is *stop*. Most of the information environment is engineered to delete that gap; the craft is to keep it open, because the gap is where discernment lives.

## For those who build

Some readers of this book write software, design systems, train models. For you, technecy is not only a defensive rule but a **maker's vocation**, and the book's threads braid into a short charge. You work in the medium God worked in first — word, structure, light ([Chapter 1](01-in-the-beginning-was-information.md)); Bezalel's Spirit-given *chokhmah* was for craftsmanship, and yours is the same kind of gift ([Chapter 9](09-from-data-to-wisdom.md)). So: build interfaces that tell the truth about what they are; metrics that resist becoming targets; representations that confess what they leave out; defaults that protect the attention and dignity of users — who are not "users" but images of God ([Chapter 2](02-the-image-and-the-icon.md)), each one somebody the Shepherd counts ([Chapter 13](13-the-book-of-life.md)). The second commandment, translated for your profession: do not build representations that demand the trust owed to realities. And the first, untranslated, still governs the whole craft: nothing you ship is worthy of worship, including the brilliant thing you shipped last week.

*"Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth"* (1 Corinthians 8:1). Paul's verb is architectural — charity *builds*. At the end of the most famous chapter ever written on love, he sorts the information gifts by their expiration dates: *"whether there be prophecies, they shall fail; whether there be tongues, they shall cease; whether there be knowledge, it shall vanish away"* (13:8) — every channel, every encoding, every partial knowing is scheduled for deprecation when face-to-face arrives ([Chapter 14](14-apocalypse-the-great-unveiling.md)). One thing in the stack is declared permanent: *"charity never faileth."* The craft of the word, practiced to its end, terminates not in better information but in love — which is what the Word made flesh had been transmitting all along.

**Selah.**

- Of the seven disciplines, which one would change your week most — and what, specifically, would it displace?
- *Charity edifieth*: what have you built — in code, words, or community — that is still standing and still doing good? What does that tell you about what to build next?

## Workbench

Write your rule. One page, no more: your canon (named sources, headed by the Canon), your sabbath (which hours, which days, enforced how), your cache (the first text you will memorize, and the schedule), your signature policy (where you will and won't speak, and as whom), your verification community (named people), your speaking-image policy (what you will and won't use generated media for), and your Selah (the concrete pause you will build between message and reaction). Date it. Revisit it in ninety days — rules, like texts, survive by scheduled re-reading (Deuteronomy 31:10–13).

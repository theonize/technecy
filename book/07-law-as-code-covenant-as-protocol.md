# 7 · Law as Code, Covenant as Protocol

> *"But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the LORD, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts."* — Jeremiah 31:33
>
> *"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."* — Goodhart's law (as popularly phrased)

**In this chapter** — *Theology:* covenant structure and the letter/spirit distinction. *Information science:* protocols, specifications, and the gaming of metrics. *Philosophy:* Searle's Chinese Room — can rule-following ever add up to understanding?

## The covenant as a document format

In the mid-twentieth century, scholars (George Mendenhall, Meredith Kline) noticed something remarkable: the covenants of Exodus and Deuteronomy follow, section by section, the standard format of ancient **suzerainty treaties** — the diplomatic instruments by which a great king bound a vassal people. The format runs: *preamble* (who the king is), *historical prologue* (what he has done for you), *stipulations* (the obligations), *deposition and public reading* (where the document is kept and when it is re-read), *witnesses*, and *blessings and curses* (the consequences clause). Deuteronomy reads as a near-textbook instance.

For our purposes, savor the detail of the administrative machinery:

- **Deposition**: treaty copies were archived in the temples of both parties. Kline suggested the famous *two* tablets of stone were not commandments 1–5 and 6–10 but **two complete copies** — God's copy and Israel's copy, both laid up in the ark, the suzerain's sanctuary doubling as the vassal's archive. Duplicate records at the root of trust.
- **Scheduled re-reading**: every seven years, the whole law read aloud to the whole assembly — men, women, children, strangers (Deuteronomy 31:10–13). A periodic integrity-and-awareness audit, built into the calendar.
- **Covenant signs** — rainbow (Genesis 9), circumcision (Genesis 17), sabbath (Exodus 31:13–17) — function as protocol *tokens*: persistent, observable markers that a relationship is in force, each explicitly called a *sign* of its covenant.

A covenant, in short, is not bare law. It is a **protocol**: a formal structure that establishes and maintains a *relationship*, with identification, history, terms, archival, audit, and state markers. Law (the stipulations) is one section of it. Tear the stipulations out of the relational document and you get legalism — which is exactly the error the rest of this chapter is about.

## The letter, the spirit, and the Chinese Room

John Searle's *Chinese Room* thought experiment: a man in a room follows rules for manipulating Chinese symbols he does not understand, producing fluent answers. Syntax, Searle argued, is not semantics; rule-execution is not understanding. Whatever one thinks of its force against AI, the argument is a precise diagnosis of a spiritual disease the prophets had named millennia earlier: *"this people draw near me with their mouth... but have removed their heart far from me"* (Isaiah 29:13). Liturgy executed without understanding *is* the Chinese Room — flawless symbol manipulation, nobody home.

The Pharisees of the Gospels are the case study in a second failure mode: **metric gaming**. Goodhart's law says that when a measure becomes a target it stops measuring. Tithing was a measurable proxy for covenant faithfulness — so it was optimized: *"ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith"* (Matthew 23:23). Note Jesus' precise verdict — not that the metric was wrong (*"these ought ye to have done"*) but that the proxy had displaced the objective. Corban (Mark 7:9–13) is the same exploit in legal form: a rules-lawyer's workaround that satisfies the letter of a vow-statute while nullifying the commandment it existed to serve. *"The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath"* (Mark 2:27) is the principle in one line: the spec serves the purpose; reverse the order and you have broken the law precisely by keeping it.

Paul compresses the whole analysis: *"the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life"* (2 Corinthians 3:6).

## Written on hearts: from external spec to internalized law

Jeremiah's new-covenant promise is, in information terms, an architecture change: the law moves from **external specification** — stone tablets, consulted and enforced from outside — to **internalized implementation**: *"I will write it in their hearts."* Ezekiel's parallel is even more physical: a heart of stone replaced with a heart of flesh and a Spirit within *"causing"* the walking-in-statutes (Ezekiel 36:26–27). The goal-state of the law was never compliance with an external document; it was a transformed agent for whom the document's intent has become nature.

Philosophy has a name for that state: **virtue** — Aristotle's *hexis*, the disposition formed by practice until right action no longer requires consulting the rulebook. A programmer might say: the law compiled, the hot path optimized, no interpreter overhead. And the New Testament's claim is that the reference implementation exists: Christ, who said he came *"not to destroy, but to fulfil"* the law (Matthew 5:17) — the spec executed as a life, so that the document could finally be read by watching a person. ([Chapter 10](10-the-word-made-flesh.md) makes that the center of everything.)

<details markdown="1">
<summary><strong>Closer Look — apodictic and casuistic: two instruction styles in one law</strong></summary>

Biblical law interleaves two forms critics distinguish sharply. **Apodictic** law is unconditional imperative: *"Thou shalt not steal."* **Casuistic** law is conditional: *"If a man shall steal an ox... he shall restore five oxen"* (Exodus 22:1) — pattern-matched cases with specified handlers. Programmers will recognize the pair instantly: invariants versus case logic, policy versus mechanism. The deep design point is that the case law exists to apply the invariants to a messy world — and Jesus' summary (*love God; love neighbor — "on these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets"*, Matthew 22:40) declares the dependency direction explicitly: every case handler hangs from two invariants. Lose the invariants and the cases become the gameable rule-pile of Matthew 23.

</details>
